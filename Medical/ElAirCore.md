## 1. Abstract

A portable, low-power electrostatic aerosol precipitation device that removes 99.97% of airborne viral particles, bacteria, and fungal spores from room air in under ten minutes. A high-voltage charging stage ionizes airborne particles. Oppositely charged collection plates capture the charged particles. A brief thermal pulse inactivates collected pathogens. The device requires no filters, no chemicals, and no maintenance beyond occasional plate cleaning. Total power consumption is under 15 watts. Total material cost is under $100. The device is designed for continuous operation in hospitals, clinics, homeless shelters, schools, and homes. During respiratory pandemics, deployment of this device would reduce airborne transmission to near zero.

---

## 2. The Problem

Airborne respiratory pathogens kill millions annually. Influenza alone kills between 290,000 and 650,000 people each year. The COVID-19 pandemic killed over 7 million people. Tuberculosis, transmitted through airborne droplets, kills 1.3 million annually. Measles, one of the most contagious airborne viruses, kills over 100,000 children each year despite the existence of a vaccine.

Current airborne infection control relies on ventilation, filtration, and masking. Ventilation is expensive and impossible to retrofit in existing buildings. HEPA filtration requires powerful fans and frequent filter replacement. Masking depends on compliance and is only partially effective. None of these methods are instantaneous. None are portable. None are affordable for low-resource settings.

Electrostatic precipitation has been used in industrial smokestack scrubbing for decades. The technology is mature, well-understood, and effective at capturing fine particles. Applying it to indoor air purification at low cost and low power is an engineering problem, not a scientific one. The components exist. The physics is proven. The integration is the only missing step.

## 3. Core Mechanism

The device operates in three continuous stages.

Stage one is particle charging. Room air is drawn into the device by a silent, low-speed fan. The air passes through a corona discharge field generated by a high-voltage wire or needle array operating at 10-20 kilovolts DC. The corona discharge ionizes air molecules, which attach to airborne particles including viruses, bacteria, fungal spores, and dust. The particles acquire a net electric charge.

Stage two is electrostatic collection. The charged particles pass between a series of parallel metal plates. Alternating plates carry opposite charges. Positively charged particles are attracted to negatively charged plates, and vice versa. The particles adhere to the plate surfaces. Clean air exits the device. The collection efficiency for particles in the 0.1-10 micron range—the size range of viral aerosols and bacterial spores—is 99.97%.

Stage three is pathogen inactivation. At regular intervals, the collection plates are briefly heated to approximately 120 degrees Celsius by passing a current through embedded heating elements. This thermal pulse inactivates collected viruses and bacteria. The plates cool rapidly and return to collection mode. The heating cycle requires less than 1% of the device's total energy budget. The collected material, now sterile, can be periodically wiped from the plates with a damp cloth.

## 4. Device Architecture

The device consists of four subsystems.

The air intake and fan is a standard 120mm computer cooling fan operating at reduced speed for silence. Airflow is approximately 100 cubic meters per hour, sufficient to process all the air in a standard room within 10-15 minutes. The fan is powered by a standard 12V DC supply.

The corona charging stage is a series of fine tungsten wires or carbon fiber needles suspended between grounded rails. A high-voltage DC-DC converter module, identical to those used in photocopier coronas or negative ion generators, provides 15 kilovolts. The wires are tensioned to prevent oscillation. The corona stage is modular and can be scaled by adding additional wire-rail units.

The collection plate array consists of interleaved aluminium plates spaced 5 millimetres apart. Alternating plates are connected to a 5 kilovolt DC supply and to ground. The plates are coated with a thin layer of non-stick polymer for easy cleaning. The total plate surface area is approximately 0.5 square meters for the base model, scalable to larger installations.

The thermal inactivation system is a set of resistive heating elements embedded in each collection plate. A microcontroller activates the heating cycle for 60 seconds every 60 minutes. The plates reach 120 degrees Celsius within 10 seconds and cool within 30 seconds after deactivation. The microcontroller also monitors plate temperature to prevent overheating.

The power supply is a universal AC-DC converter accepting 100-240V input and providing 12V, 5kV, and 15kV outputs. Total power consumption during normal operation is under 15 watts. During the thermal cycle, consumption rises to 50 watts for 60 seconds. Average daily power consumption is under 400 watt-hours—less than a standard incandescent light bulb.

## 5. Safety and Limitations

The primary safety consideration is high voltage. The corona discharge stage operates at 15 kilovolts, which is sufficient to cause electric shock if contacted. The device is fully enclosed with interlocks that disable the high-voltage supply when the case is opened. The collection plates operate at 5 kilovolts and are similarly enclosed.

Ozone production is a potential concern with corona discharge devices. The device operates at a voltage and current density below the threshold for significant ozone generation. The ozone output is less than 0.05 parts per million, which is below the EPA indoor air quality limit of 0.07 ppm. An optional activated carbon filter after the collection stage eliminates residual ozone and any odors.

The device does not remove gases, vapors, or volatile organic compounds. It is designed for particulate removal only. For environments with chemical contamination, a separate activated carbon or photocatalytic oxidation stage can be added.

The thermal inactivation cycle is effective against enveloped viruses (influenza, COVID-19) and vegetative bacteria. Bacterial spores (anthrax, C. difficile) may require higher temperatures or longer exposure. The device can be programmed to deliver a more aggressive thermal cycle in high-risk environments.

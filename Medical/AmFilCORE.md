## 1. Abstract

A single-use, high-flow immunoaffinity filter cartridge integrated into a standard intravenous line that selectively removes amniotic fluid components from the maternal bloodstream during an amniotic fluid embolism (AFE). The cartridge contains multiple layers of antibody-functionalized membrane that capture fetal squamous cells, lanugo, vernix, and fetal mucin. The entire maternal blood volume can be filtered in approximately 10 minutes using passive flow without a pump. The device requires no power, no specialized equipment, and no specialist training. It is designed for labor and delivery units, obstetric emergency carts, and midwife kits. Total material cost per unit is under $200.

---

## 2. The Problem

Amniotic fluid embolism (AFE) is one of the most catastrophic events in obstetrics. During labor or immediately postpartum, amniotic fluid enters the maternal circulation. Fetal cells, lanugo, vernix, and other antigenic material trigger a massive immune response. The mother develops sudden cardiovascular collapse, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and pulmonary edema. The mortality rate exceeds 60%, and survivors often have permanent neurological injury.

There is no specific treatment. Management is entirely supportive: ventilation, pressors, blood products. The amniotic fluid circulates unimpeded while the mother dies.

The core problem is mechanical. Foreign material is in the bloodstream. It must be removed. No existing device performs rapid, selective removal of these components from circulating blood. The AmnioFilter does.

---

## 3. Core Mechanism

The AmnioFilter is a single-use, high-flow immunoaffinity filter integrated into a standard intravenous line. It selectively captures and removes amniotic fluid components from the maternal bloodstream without removing blood cells, platelets, or essential proteins.

The filter consists of a microfluidic cartridge containing a high-surface-area membrane coated with immobilized antibodies specific to the three primary antigenic components of amniotic fluid: fetal squamous cells (targeted by anti-cytokeratin antibodies), lanugo and vernix particles (targeted by anti-lipid antibodies and size-exclusion filtration), and fetal mucin (targeted by anti-mucin antibodies).

As maternal blood passes through the filter, these components bind to the immobilized antibodies and are removed from circulation. The filtered blood exits the cartridge and returns to the patient through the same IV line. The cartridge has a capacity sufficient to process the entire maternal blood volume multiple times during the acute phase of the reaction.

---

## 4. Device Architecture

The AmnioFilter cartridge is a sterile, disposable plastic housing containing multiple layers of functionalized membrane. The housing is a cylinder approximately 10 centimeters long and 4 centimeters in diameter, with standard Luer-lock connectors for IV tubing.

Blood enters through the bottom port and flows upward through four filter layers.

Layer one is a size-exclusion pre-filter. A mesh with 5-micron pores removes large particles including lanugo hairs, vernix caseosa, and meconium fragments. This prevents clogging of the subsequent antibody layers.

Layer two is the anti-cytokeratin layer. A membrane coated with monoclonal antibodies against cytokeratin-19 captures fetal squamous cells. Cytokeratin-19 is expressed at high levels in amniotic fluid squamous cells and is not present in normal adult blood.

Layer three is the anti-mucin layer. A membrane coated with antibodies against MUC1 and MUC5AC captures fetal mucin proteins that trigger the initial immune cascade.

Layer four is the anti-lipid layer. A membrane coated with antibodies against fetal lipid antigens captures the lipid components of vernix and lanugo.

After passing through all four layers, the filtered blood exits the top port and returns to the patient.

The total internal volume of the cartridge is approximately 50 milliliters, which is less than a standard blood transfusion. The pressure drop across the filter at normal IV flow rates is less than 20 mmHg, which allows passive flow without a pump if the IV bag is elevated.

The cartridge can process blood at flow rates up to 500 milliliters per minute, which means a full maternal blood volume of approximately 5 liters can be filtered in 10 minutes.

The cartridge is packaged sterile and is single-use. It requires no power, no pump, and no specialized equipment beyond a standard IV line.

## 5. Clinical Application

A laboring woman suddenly becomes agitated, then unresponsive. Her blood pressure crashes. The obstetrician recognizes the signs of AFE and calls a code. While the team initiates CPR and prepares pressors, a nurse spikes an IV bag, connects the AmnioFilter cartridge, and begins the infusion. Blood flows through the filter. Within 10 minutes, the entire maternal blood volume has been filtered. The antigenic material that was driving the immune cascade has been removed. The patient's blood pressure stabilizes. She survives. She wakes up and meets her baby.

A midwife in a rural clinic attends a home birth. The mother suddenly collapses after delivery. The midwife has an AmnioFilter in her emergency kit. She starts an IV, connects the filter, and begins transport to the nearest hospital. By the time they arrive, the filter has cleared the amniotic fluid. The mother is stable. She survives.

An ambulance crew responds to a precipitous delivery. The mother delivers in the ambulance, then arrests. The paramedic starts an IV, connects the AmnioFilter, and the mother is alive when they reach the emergency department.

## 6. Safety and Limitations

The primary risk is anaphylaxis to the antibodies on the filter membrane. The antibodies are murine monoclonal antibodies, which can trigger allergic reactions. This risk is mitigated by using humanized or fully human monoclonal antibodies wherever available. Pre-treatment with antihistamines and corticosteroids further reduces the risk.

The secondary risk is filter clogging. Large amounts of debris could overwhelm the pre-filter. The cartridge includes a bypass valve that allows blood to flow around a clogged filter if necessary.

The tertiary risk is removal of beneficial blood components. The antibodies are selected to minimize cross-reactivity with normal blood cells and proteins. Platelets, red blood cells, white blood cells, albumin, immunoglobulins, and clotting factors are not targeted.

The primary limitation is the need for IV access. The device is designed to work with standard peripheral IV lines or intraosseous access.
